# Tournament match flow

Enter Play in `Assets/Scenes/TCGBoard.unity`. `TowerMatchController` presents five 3D floors, rolls two six-sided dice with automatic tie rerolls, and starts the higher roller. The human controls Orren. The second floor deliberately says **The Sleepless Eye vs ?**, as requested; higher pairings remain **? vs ?**.

Known tower leaders display their existing card artwork in framed portrait slots with names underneath: Orren Dawnbringer and The Sleepless Eye on floor one, and The Sleepless Eye on floor two. Unrevealed slots retain a question mark. Portraits resolve through the same card-art library as the board avatars and preserve the artwork's proportions.

## Controls

- Replenish continues automatically after the last draw animation. Any stage without a legal action advances automatically, including empty Events and Recover Objects. **Next stage** is shown only while you have optional actions remaining.
- Settlements (population centres) are never drawn or played: every PC in the deck sits in a pool from the first turn, and each company starts the match at a random one of its own. During **Select Destination** (after Realms) a popup shows every settlement whose land is on the board — held by you or by your opponent, your own towns and the rest of the world's — as a pile, one face at a time, sorted by how many hand cards each town would let you play (most first), then by side (your own before neutral before hostile), then by distance (closest first); the best one is shown on opening. Browse with the arrows (or ← →), **Travel here** to set out, or **Stay**. The face is framed by how the town would receive you (teal: welcome, orange: neutral, red: hostile); beneath it a line names up to two of the hand cards you could play there ("among others" past two), a hostile or neutral town adds a line saying its dwellers must be beaten first, and the road is drawn as a chain of stops. The PC face itself reads `PC. Region (Side)`, then the dwellers, that characters born there may be recruited, and the object types it trades in, with the quote last. The **DESTINATION** zone only ever shows the one token you are at.
- **Travel** follows. The road is walked one region at a time; the banner under the stage bar shows the company marker moving along the stops. Each stop draws a card by its position on the road (1 Land, 2 Event or Action, 3 Encounter, 4 Army, 5 Character — or another Army when the deck has no character left; a missing kind falls back to the top card; stops past the fifth draw nothing). Then the *other* company may fall on you with its characters and with armies whose **terrain** matches the region's — from its field (untapped units) **or straight from its hand**: a hand card fights that one stop, then is shuffled back into its owner's deck whatever happened to it. You defend with untapped field units under the same terrain rule. Unblocked damage hits your life. Staying still is a one-stop road through the region you are in, so there is always at least one chance for an attack. When the enemy travels, you are the raider: click glowing units or hand cards, then **Attack!** or **Let them pass**. When you travel, choose a defender, then the attacker it meets; the toggle decides whether defenders **tap** (full strength) or **stand fast** (stay untapped, fight at -2/-2, so they can still face the dwellers on arrival). **Resolve combat** declines further blocks.
- Every land and army has a terrain (Plains, Forest, Hills, Mountains, Marsh, Desert, Coast, Wasteland); characters fight anywhere. The map's borders, terrains and coordinates are in `Docs/CaldrathMap.md` and `Assets/Resources/RegionMap.json`.
- On arrival a settlement's **standing** applies. Your own deck's settlements, and those of your side, are safe. A neutral settlement (or one both sides' decks list) is held by its **dwellers**, an army named on the PC face (hover it for the card): during Muster click a ready unit for a *retention attack* — a dice duel against the dwellers; win and you may act there; lose (or tie) and both the unit and the town tap for the turn, no blood spilt. A settlement of the other side is hostile: the duel is for real, a loss lands on your unit (killed, wounded or tapped by the margin), and winning opens the town. The token reads **HELD BY DWELLERS** / **CLOSED** meanwhile.
- Characters, encounters and objects are played at the destination and only there: a character or encounter must be born there (`startingPC` / `birthplaces`), an object must be of a kind the settlement trades in (`objectType` against the PC's `objectTypes`). One such play taps the destination for the rest of the turn (it shows **USED**); Undo untaps it. Armies deploy anywhere. **Recruits enter tapped** (shown **MUSTERING**) and ready at their owner's next turn start, so they sit out the enemy's next road; Mounted troops enter ready.
- Tapping a settlement while the opponent holds a character or encounter *in hand* born there lets them answer: a character enters their field for free, strikes the unit that acted (or your weakest) and taps; an encounter is sprung on you through `ResolveEncounter`. The human answers by clicking the glowing card or **Let them pass**; the opponent answers with its best hitter. If both companies stand at the same settlement and both have tapped it, the two champions (the unit that acted there, else the hardest hitter) trade blows at once.
- **Duels** decide every clash (road blocks, dwellers, ambushes, meetings): attack + d6 each, the winner deals the difference; over the loser's defense kills, equal wounds, under just taps, a tie does nothing. The dice show in the travel banner. A unit already tapped when it fights acts at **-1/-1**. Armies can be killed; characters never die — a killing blow **wounds** them instead (shown upside down, out of action, unable to attack, defend, fight dwellers or pick up spoils). A wounded unit heals at the end of its owner's turn if it carries an object that heals or the company rests at one of its own settlements; it comes back tapped and sits out the next untap (**HEALING**).
- At the end of the turn the hand must be back at its limit (5): **Recover Objects** asks you to discard the surplus before the turn can hand over.
- Pin a hand card and select **Play card** during its deployment stage. Objects prompt for a friendly character. An encounter's face only says where it can be investigated; investigating it spends the card (`MatchRules.ResolveEncounter` is the outcome hook, and an unregistered outcome still allows the play).
- Click a glowing land during Muster or Events to tap it for mana (or pin it and use **Tap land**). Mana can be gathered as cards are needed; Realms never allows land tapping. The opponent gathers mana during deployment as needed. Muster and Events remain available when ready lands can fund a valid card, and empty stages still skip.
- Click your armies/characters during stage 5 to commit attacks. Cards tagged `ChooseTarget` prompt for an enemy target.
- Stage 7 prompts for each dead character's object. Select a surviving friendly character, or offer it to the enemy and select an eligible enemy. If neither side has a character, the object is discarded.
- The opponent plays automatically, except that the human chooses human blockers and human object recipients. It travels to the settlement that the most cards in its hand can be played at, staying on a tie.
- A pulsing mint border identifies legal cards/tokens for the current action, including object recipients, attack targets and blockers. Illegal actions have no button in the preview. New non-Mounted units enter tapped and show **MUSTERING**; Mounted ones enter ready.

The tower reuses the Golden Vale's original limestone, sunlit stone, ink, blue slate and verdigris materials. Dice use dedicated fine-grain ivory and slate surfaces derived from that palette, with polished gold details instead of enlarged masonry textures. Board updates preserve surviving card-view instances: only cards whose tapped state changes animate, and only the new turn owner's non-Halted units untap. Material counters read the current match pools, including after a match reset.

## Rules implemented

1. Refill the active hand to its `HandLimit` (default 5; accepts 6/7), ready cards except Halted, wounded and healing ones (recruits from last turn ready here) (the destination untaps too, and a destination that is not the company's own or its side's is held by its dwellers again).
2. Clear previous environmental cards; deploy lands and environments.
3. Select Destination: pick any known settlement whose region's land is on the board (a land card may be an alternate for a region, see `landAliases`), or stay. One choice per turn; the destination itself only changes on arrival.
4. Travel: the road is the shortest route on the region map from the current region to the destination's, one stop per region entered (one stop, the current region, when staying or without a map). Each stop draws its typed card, then the other player declares attacks with ready field units and with hand cards that fight on that ground (characters always; armies whose terrain matches); hand raiders leave the field after the stop and are shuffled into the deck, never falling or wounding. The traveller assigns untapped field defenders under the same rule, each either tapping (full strength) or standing fast (untapped, -2/-2). Every clash is a **duel**: each side adds a d6 to its attack; the higher total wins and deals the difference to the loser — more than the loser's defense kills, equal wounds, less merely taps; equal totals are a stand-off. An attacker duels each of its blockers in turn while it still stands. Unblocked attacks reduce the traveller's life by the attack value, no dice. Attackers tap. The company arrives after the last stop.
5. Tap lands as needed for the existing seven materials, serving as mana. Pay printed costs for characters, armies, objects and encounters; recruited armies and characters enter tapped unless Mounted. Characters and encounters must be born at the destination, objects must be of a kind it trades in, and objects attach only to characters. A destination held by its dwellers must be secured first (retention attack on neutral ground, normal attack on hostile ground). The first play taps the destination, which may trigger the opponent's ambush from hand and, if both companies hold the same town, a clash of champions; armies ignore the destination.
6. Event stage and an explicit effect execution hook.
7. Resolve objects from fallen armies, discard down to the hand limit, heal wounded characters that can, then hand over the turn. The ending player's untapped lands automatically tap and grant their materials. Both mana pools persist across turns; the next player's lands untap as usual.

Combat values are the card face's, including troop/character fallback values, minus 1/1 while the unit is already tapped and minus 2/2 for a defender standing fast. Characters never die: a killing blow wounds them. Life defaults to 20 as a configurable prototype win condition. An empty deck currently draws no cards; it does not itself cause a loss.

## Content boundaries

The human plays Orren Dawnbringer using the `orren_the_kindled` deck against `the_sleepless_eye`. Match and tower labels use Orren; the avatar displays his full name, Orren Dawnbringer.

`useStarterDeck` selects the implemented deployment card types (lands, environments, characters, armies, objects, encounters) and gives an opening with lands and an inexpensive army; settlements are split out into the pool before the deck is built. It preserves card identities and costs. This is a development deck, not a balanced final deck. Disable the option to use the complete catalog deck.

Existing events describe the older hex-board game; their executable effects are absent. Unsupported events cannot be spent or discarded by playing them. Register `MatchRules.ResolveEvent` to implement effects; the starter selection excludes these cards until their effects are authored. Action, Spell and Ally cards likewise have no stage in the requested flow and are excluded from the starter selection.

The full keyword system (first strike, flying restrictions, triggered/probabilistic effects, ongoing status damage, equipment bonuses, etc.) remains to be implemented; this is the basic combat and turn-flow foundation. Location is the destination plus the road to it: the route is read from the region map, not from lands on the board. Settlement alignment and dwellers are derived by `AgentScripts/assign_settlements.py` (single-alignment deck lists → that side; mixed or none → Neutral), terrains by `AgentScripts/assign_terrains.py`, and the map by `AgentScripts/build_region_map.py`. Later floor opponent identities/decks are intentionally unauthored. A first-floor victory returns to the tower and reports that boundary, rather than inventing a next match.

## Validation

`Tests/ManaCounterChecks.cs` verifies all fourteen visible material counters after human/opponent land taps, prevents duplicate grants, checks spending and mana carryover, and verifies binding to replacement match pools. Run after the opening in Play mode, then restart Play.

`unity command eval_file --file Tests/MatchRulesChecks.cs` checks stage gates, refill to seven, Halted, land/PC prerequisites, attachment validation, unsupported-event preservation, road attacks with summon timing, tapping, multiple blockers, simultaneous damage, mandatory spoils, the hand-limit discard and turn handoff. `Tests/SettlementChecks.cs` covers standing, dwellers fights, wounds and healing, the tapped -1/-1, ambushes from hand, two companies meeting, map routes, stop-by-stop roads with typed draws, terrain-gated road attacks and the end-of-turn discard. Live Play mode checks cover the cinematic transition, opponent initiative, board synchronization and UI captures. The cinematic uses `TournamentRenderer.asset` through a dedicated camera; the pipeline's default renderer stays unchanged.

`Tests/MatchEdgeChecks.cs` also passes targeted combat, environmental cleanup, no-recipient object recovery, printed stat fallback and lethal victory. `Tests/MatchBoardChecks.cs` passes the live board-to-rules integration checks (run in Play mode after the opening); it disables automatic opponent updates for deterministic testing, so restart Play afterward.

`Tests/MatchStageFeedbackChecks.cs` validates read-only legality, stage availability, new-unit readiness, mana ownership and untap boundaries. `Tests/MatchFeedbackLiveChecks.cs` checks automatic advancement, highlighted cards, removal of illegal preview buttons, direct attacks and preservation of token identities/rotations across actions and stages. The coroutine writes its result to `Temp/MatchFeedbackLiveResult.txt`; restart Play after running it.

Realms advances through Select Destination and Travel to Muster: there is no separate Gather Mana stage, and no attack stages of their own. `Tests/ManaTimingChecks.cs` checks this transition and funding during Muster and Events. `Tests/DestinationChecks.cs` covers the settlement pool, land-gated choices, the single destination token, destination-gated plays and tapping, undo, persistence across turns and the opponent's travel preference.
