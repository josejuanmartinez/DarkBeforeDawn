# Tournament match flow

Enter Play in `Assets/Scenes/TCGBoard.unity`. `TowerMatchController` presents five 3D floors, rolls two six-sided dice with automatic tie rerolls, and starts the higher roller. The human controls Orren. The second floor deliberately says **The Sleepless Eye vs ?**, as requested; higher pairings remain **? vs ?**.

Known tower leaders display their existing card artwork in framed portrait slots with names underneath: Orren Dawnbringer and The Sleepless Eye on floor one, and The Sleepless Eye on floor two. Unrevealed slots retain a question mark. Portraits resolve through the same card-art library as the board avatars and preserve the artwork's proportions.

## Controls

- Replenish continues automatically after the last draw animation. Any stage without a legal action advances automatically, including empty Events and Recover Objects. **Next stage** is shown only while you have optional actions remaining.
- Settlements (population centres) are never drawn or played: every PC in the deck sits in a pool from the first turn, and each company starts the match at a random one of its own. During **Select Destination** (after Realms) a popup shows every settlement whose land is on the board — held by you or by your opponent, your own towns and the rest of the world's — as a pile, one face at a time, sorted by how many hand cards each town would let you play (most first), then by side (your own before neutral before hostile), then by distance (closest first); the best one is shown on opening. Browse with the arrows (or ← →), **Travel here** to set out, or **Stay**. The face is framed by how the town would receive you (teal: welcome, orange: neutral, red: hostile); beneath it a large line names up to three of the hand cards you could play there ("among others" past three), each name an underlined link that shows the card on hover; a hostile or neutral town adds a line saying its dwellers must be beaten first. Beside the face, the map of Caldrath (`Assets/Resources/Maps/Caldrath.png`, region markers in `RegionMap.json` as `mapX`/`mapY`) is framed on the company and every town on offer: the road from where the company stands is drawn on it, one terrain-tinted stop per region entered with the card it draws (`+1 ⟨glyph⟩`; hover it for the rule), the destination ringed and named, and every other region on offer ringed in gold — clicking one browses to a town there. Hovering any region's painted terrain symbol names the region and its ground, shows the ground's glyph, and says what that ground means for a company crossing it, naming the kinds of army that fight there (wolves and spiders in the forest, dwarves and goblins in the mountains, and so on — `CardKeywordGlossary.TerrainHost`, drawn from the catalog's armies); a land card's `Plains ground ⟨glyph⟩` line and an army's `Ambush: ⟨glyph⟩` line explain the same on hover. The PC face itself reads `PC. Region (Side)` — the region underlined, a link that shows the region's land card on hover — then the dwellers (a link to the army), that characters born there may be recruited, and the object types it trades in, with the quote last. The **DESTINATION** zone only ever shows the one token you are at.
- **Travel** follows, in an opaque popup over the upper half of the board: it covers the enemy's lanes and the environment and leaves your own army lane and hand clear. The map takes the left of the popup, framed on the road; the road is walked one region at a time, the company marker (the champion's portrait) moving from stop to stop on the map, passed legs lit in the company's colour, each stop labelled with what it pays (`+1 ⟨glyph⟩`, hoverable for the rule) and the destination ringed and named. The right column shows the region entered, its ground (hover the badge for the ground's glyph and the armies that fight there), the phase line and one lane of the *enemy's* units as chips: when you travel, the raiders committed against you (tagged with who meets them) and, while the enemy weighs its ambush, what could still fall on you; when the enemy travels, its defenders that can stand on this ground, tagged with what they have taken on. Your own units are never chips: the ones that may act glow on the board and in your hand and are clicked there (the enemy's hand stays hidden). Glowing enemy chips are clickable when a click on the covered board card would count — the attacker your chosen defender meets. While any popup is up (travel, destination picker, combat screen) nothing on the board is hovered or previewed at all: the glow says what can be clicked. Each stop draws a card by its position on the road (1 Land, 2 Event or Action, 3 Encounter, 4 Army, 5 Character — or another Army when the deck has no character left; a missing kind falls back to the top card; stops past the fifth draw nothing). Then the *other* company may fall on you with its characters and with armies whose **terrain** matches the region's — from its field (untapped units) **or straight from its hand**: a hand card fights that one stop, then is shuffled back into its owner's deck whatever happened to it. You defend with untapped field units under the same terrain rule. Unblocked damage hits your life. Staying still is a one-stop road through the region you are in, so there is always at least one chance for an attack. When the enemy travels, you are the raider: click glowing units or hand cards, then **Attack!** or **Let them pass**. When you travel, choose a defender, then the attacker it meets; the toggle decides whether defenders **tap** (full strength) or **stand fast** (stay untapped, fight at -2/-2, so they can still face the dwellers on arrival). **Resolve combat** declines further blocks.
- Every land and army has a terrain (Plains, Forest, Hills, Mountains, Marsh, Desert, Coast, Wasteland); characters fight anywhere. The map's borders, terrains, layout coordinates and marker positions on the drawn map are in `Docs/CaldrathMap.md` and `Assets/Resources/RegionMap.json` (both generated by `AgentScripts/build_region_map.py`); `CaldrathMapView` frames the picture and lays routes over it in both popups.
- **Arrival** follows the road whenever the company has something to do at the gates: a ready (untapped, unwounded) character or army, and either dwellers to beat or hand cards it could play in town. With neither, play goes straight on to Muster. Arrival is for the gates only: nothing is played from the hand there (armies included) and no land is tapped; a hand card's hover says so and points to Muster. On arrival a settlement's **standing** applies. Your own deck's settlements, and those of your side, are safe. A neutral settlement (or one both sides' decks list) is held by its **dwellers**, an army named on the PC face (hover it for the card): click a ready unit for a *retention attack* — a dice duel against the dwellers; win and the unit walks in; lose (or tie) and both the unit and the town tap for the turn, no blood spilt. A settlement of the other side is hostile: the duel is for real, a loss lands on your unit (killed, wounded or tapped by the margin), and winning lets the unit walk in. The token reads **HELD BY DWELLERS** / **CLOSED** meanwhile.
- With **nothing ready** (every unit tapped, or no unit at all) the company can still act: the **champion leads** (button under the stage bar). At a welcome town it simply walks the company in. At a held town it faces the dwellers: on the field (even tapped, at -1/-1) a lost duel wounds it; not yet played, it fights as a stand-in with the avatar's printed numbers and a lost hostile duel costs the company's life the margin instead (retention duels stay bloodless). A company with no champion card known cannot fight for a held town.
- A welcome (or beaten) town still has to be **entered**: click a ready unit (**ENTER {town}**); it taps, the town taps (**ENTERED**), and for the rest of the turn characters, encounters and objects can be played there — as many as you can pay for. A company with every unit tapped cannot enter, so a defender that tapped on the road has traded the town for the fight; **stand fast** keeps it ready for the gates. **Stay outside** skips it; entering is still possible from Muster.
- Characters, encounters and objects are played at the entered destination and only there: a character or encounter must be born there (`startingPC` / `birthplaces`), an object must be of a kind the settlement trades in (`objectType` against the PC's `objectTypes`). Armies deploy anywhere. **Recruits enter tapped** (shown **MUSTERING**) and ready at their owner's next turn start, so they sit out the enemy's next road; Mounted troops enter ready.
- Entering a settlement while the opponent holds a character or encounter *in hand* born there lets them answer: a character enters their field for free, strikes the unit that walked in and taps; an encounter is sprung on you through `ResolveEncounter`. The human answers by clicking the glowing card or **Let them pass**; the opponent answers with its best hitter. If both companies stand at the same settlement and both have entered it, the two champions (the unit that walked in, else the hardest hitter) trade blows at once.
- Every resolution — a stop's fight, the dwellers, an ambush, a meeting — opens the **combat screen** on a black ground: *Combat at {settlement}* or *Combat in {region}* under a wash of the ground's colour, the region and ground (a `terrain:` link) in the subtitle, both companies' life in the corners, and one row per attacker: its portrait (lit gold when it won its duel, dimmed red when it fell) against each unit that stood in its way (**FROM HAND**, **STANDS FAST**, **DWELLERS** tagged), the pip dice tumbling until they settle on the roll, the totals struck up, the blow stamped across the duel (**KILLED** / **STRUCK DOWN** / **WOUNDED** / **TAPPED** / **STAND-OFF**) with a burst of light on whoever took it, and the margin against defense beneath; an unblocked attacker shows the life it took. The board's own battle sparks fade out while it is up. Rows deal in one by one; a click or key deals them all, the next dismisses. The match waits until it is dismissed.
- The hand is dealt every type in the deck. Actions and spells have no stage yet, and events wait on `ResolveEvent`: such a card can be **set aside** (discarded) during Muster so it does not clog the hand; the opponent sheds its own after playing.
- Environmental cards name the sides they touch, one line per side glyph. Every character and army of a named side (by its company's side) carries the weather: a breathing ring in the environment's colour, motes rising through its frame, a badge per environment with its glyph, and the line itself on its inspection card. The environmental token wears the ring alone. This is presentation only; the effects themselves are not yet executed.
- **Duels** decide every clash (road blocks, dwellers, ambushes, meetings): attack + d6 each, the winner deals the difference; over the loser's defense kills, equal wounds, under just taps, a tie does nothing. The dice show in the travel banner. A unit already tapped when it fights acts at **-1/-1**. Armies can be killed; characters never die — a killing blow **wounds** them instead (shown upside down, out of action, unable to attack, defend, fight dwellers or pick up spoils). A wounded unit heals at the end of its owner's turn if it carries an object that heals or the company rests at one of its own settlements; it comes back tapped and sits out the next untap (**HEALING**).
- At the end of the turn the hand must be back at its limit (5): **Recover Objects** asks you to discard the surplus before the turn can hand over.
- Click a hand card to play it during its deployment stage (the hover preview says **CLICK TO PLAY CARD** when it is legal, **CLICK TO TAP LANDS & PLAY** when your ready lands would pay for it, or why it cannot be played — a shortfall is named: "Needs 2 leather and 1 gold more: your ready lands can pay it" or "... 3 timber even with every ready land tapped"). A card the ready lands can pay for glows like any legal card; clicking it taps the lands it needs (`MatchRules.GatherFor`: the lands covering most of what is still owed first, never a land that brings the card no closer) and plays it. Objects prompt for a friendly character. An encounter's face says where it can be faced — "Face this encounter at X with your company, or force your opponent's company at X into it", each town a link to its card; playing it there spends the card (`MatchRules.ResolveEncounter` is the outcome hook, and an unregistered outcome still allows the play).
- Click a glowing land during Muster or Events to tap it for mana by hand. Mana can be gathered as cards are needed; Realms and Arrival never allow land tapping. The opponent gathers mana during deployment as needed. Muster and Events remain available when ready lands can fund a valid card, and empty stages still skip.
- Click your armies/characters during stage 5 to commit attacks. Cards tagged `ChooseTarget` prompt for an enemy target.
- Stage 7 prompts for each dead character's object. Select a surviving friendly character, or offer it to the enemy and select an eligible enemy. If neither side has a character, the object is discarded.
- The opponent plays automatically, except that the human chooses human blockers and human object recipients. It travels to the settlement that the most cards in its hand can be played at, staying on a tie.
- A card or token that may act is unmistakable: a bright breathing gold frame, a charged aura with a halo of light and rising embers, and in the hand it rises out of the row and rocks gently. This marks every legal action, including object recipients, attack targets and blockers. Illegal actions have no button in the preview. New non-Mounted units enter tapped and show **MUSTERING**; Mounted ones enter ready.

- The champion's card is a portrait: it shows no cost, and its class levels and combat numbers appear only once the character has been played. The company's life is a blood-red bar stacked between the title and the artwork of that card, with clear ground on both sides so it never covers the picture (it flashes and shakes when hit, the wound drains away as a bright trail, the heart glyph beats faster as life runs low); there is no separate health widget. Card backs are the deck emblems under `Resources/DeckBacks` (one per deck id, lifted from Runeboard's `Art/Decks`): the draw deck shows yours, drawn cards travel face down with it, and a card face falls back to it for its deck badge.
- Each board panel wears a blind-stamped device for what it holds: peaks for lands, a keep for settlements, crossed swords for armies, a fanned stack for the draw deck, crossbones for the discard, a sun for the environment lane, a fan of cards for the hand, an ingot seal for materials, a star for the champion (`BoardEmblem`).
- The tower climb (and its pause) can be skipped with a click or any key; the camera jumps to the current floor.

The tower reuses the Golden Vale's original limestone, sunlit stone, ink, blue slate and verdigris materials. Dice use dedicated fine-grain ivory and slate surfaces derived from that palette, with polished gold details instead of enlarged masonry textures. Board updates preserve surviving card-view instances: only cards whose tapped state changes animate, and only the new turn owner's non-Halted units untap. Material counters read the current match pools, including after a match reset.

## Rules implemented

1. Refill the active hand to its `HandLimit` (default 5; accepts 6/7), ready cards except Halted, wounded and healing ones (recruits from last turn ready here) (the destination untaps too — it must be entered again — and a destination that is not the company's own or its side's is held by its dwellers again).
2. Clear previous environmental cards; deploy lands and environments.
3. Select Destination: pick any known settlement whose region's land is on the board (a land card may be an alternate for a region, see `landAliases`), or stay. One choice per turn; the destination itself only changes on arrival.
4. Travel: the road is the shortest route on the region map from the current region to the destination's, one stop per region entered (one stop, the current region, when staying or without a map). Each stop draws its typed card, then the other player declares attacks with ready field units and with hand cards that fight on that ground (characters always; armies whose terrain matches); hand raiders leave the field after the stop and are shuffled into the deck, never falling or wounding. The traveller assigns untapped field defenders under the same rule, each either tapping (full strength) or standing fast (untapped, -2/-2). Every clash is a **duel**: each side adds a d6 to its attack; the higher total wins and deals the difference to the loser — more than the loser's defense kills, equal wounds, less merely taps; equal totals are a stand-off. An attacker duels each of its blockers in turn while it still stands. Unblocked attacks reduce the traveller's life by the attack value, no dice. Attackers tap. The company arrives after the last stop.
5. Arrival: a ready unit secures the destination if its dwellers hold it (retention attack on neutral ground, normal attack on hostile ground) and walks in, or walks into a welcome one; the unit and the town tap. Entering may trigger the opponent's ambush from hand and, if both companies hold the same town, a clash of champions. The stage only opens when there is a ready unit and something to do; entering is also allowed from Muster. No card is played and no land tapped during Arrival.
6. Muster: tap lands as needed for the existing seven materials, serving as mana (by hand, or automatically when a card the ready lands can pay for is clicked). Pay printed costs for characters, armies, objects and encounters; recruited armies and characters enter tapped unless Mounted. Characters and encounters must be born at the entered destination, objects must be of a kind it trades in, and objects attach only to characters; armies ignore the destination. Actions, spells and events without a registered effect may be set aside.
7. Event stage and an explicit effect execution hook.
8. Resolve objects from fallen armies, discard down to the hand limit, heal wounded characters that can, then hand over the turn. The ending player's untapped lands automatically tap and grant their materials. Both mana pools persist across turns; the next player's lands untap as usual.

Combat values are the card face's, including troop/character fallback values, minus 1/1 while the unit is already tapped and minus 2/2 for a defender standing fast. Characters never die: a killing blow wounds them. Life defaults to 20 as a configurable prototype win condition. An empty deck currently draws no cards; it does not itself cause a loss.

## Content boundaries

The human plays Orren Dawnbringer using the `orren_the_kindled` deck against `the_sleepless_eye`. Match and tower labels use Orren; the avatar displays his full name, Orren Dawnbringer.

`useStarterDeck` deals every card type the deck lists (settlements are split out into the pool before the deck is built) and gives an opening with lands and an inexpensive army. It preserves card identities and costs. This is a development deck, not a balanced final deck. Disable the option to use the complete catalog deck.

Existing events describe the older hex-board game; their executable effects are absent. Unsupported events cannot be spent or discarded by playing them. Register `MatchRules.ResolveEvent` to implement effects; until then they are dead weight in the hand and can be set aside during Muster. Action and Spell cards likewise have no stage in the requested flow and can be set aside the same way.

The full keyword system (first strike, flying restrictions, triggered/probabilistic effects, ongoing status damage, equipment bonuses, etc.) remains to be implemented; this is the basic combat and turn-flow foundation. Location is the destination plus the road to it: the route is read from the region map, not from lands on the board. Settlement alignment and dwellers are derived by `AgentScripts/assign_settlements.py` (single-alignment deck lists → that side; mixed or none → Neutral), terrains by `AgentScripts/assign_terrains.py`, and the map by `AgentScripts/build_region_map.py`. Later floor opponent identities/decks are intentionally unauthored. A first-floor victory returns to the tower and reports that boundary, rather than inventing a next match.

## Validation

`Tests/ManaCounterChecks.cs` verifies all fourteen visible material counters after human/opponent land taps, prevents duplicate grants, checks spending and mana carryover, and verifies binding to replacement match pools. Run after the opening in Play mode, then restart Play.

`unity command eval_file --file Tests/MatchRulesChecks.cs` checks stage gates, refill to seven, Halted, land/PC prerequisites, attachment validation, unsupported-event preservation, road attacks with summon timing, tapping, multiple blockers, simultaneous damage, mandatory spoils, the hand-limit discard and turn handoff. `Tests/SettlementChecks.cs` covers standing, dwellers fights, wounds and healing, the tapped -1/-1, ambushes from hand, two companies meeting, map routes, stop-by-stop roads with typed draws, terrain-gated road attacks and the end-of-turn discard. Live Play mode checks cover the cinematic transition, opponent initiative, board synchronization and UI captures; `Tests/TravelPresentationChecks.cs` (after `AgentScripts/PreviewBoardPresentation.cs`) and `Tests/DestinationMapChecks.cs` cover the map in the Travel and Select Destination popups, and `AgentScripts/CaptureCaldrathMap.cs` stages both for a capture (`Docs/Caldrath-Destination.png`); `AgentScripts/StageRoadFight.cs` stages a raided road with the human attacking, defending, or the fight resolved into the combat screen (`Docs/Caldrath-Travel.png`, `Docs/Combat-Screen.png`), and `AgentScripts/StageMuster.cs` a Muster where a click on a glowing card gathers its lands (both read a mode from `Temp/fx_mode.txt`). The cinematic uses `TournamentRenderer.asset` through a dedicated camera; the pipeline's default renderer stays unchanged.

`Tests/EnvironmentChecks.cs` covers the per-side reading of environmental text and which field units the weather touches. `Tests/MatchEdgeChecks.cs` also passes targeted combat, environmental cleanup, no-recipient object recovery, printed stat fallback and lethal victory. `Tests/MatchBoardChecks.cs` passes the live board-to-rules integration checks (run in Play mode after the opening); it disables automatic opponent updates for deterministic testing, so restart Play afterward.

`Tests/MatchStageFeedbackChecks.cs` validates read-only legality, stage availability, new-unit readiness, mana ownership and untap boundaries. `Tests/MatchFeedbackLiveChecks.cs` checks automatic advancement, highlighted cards, removal of illegal preview buttons, direct attacks and preservation of token identities/rotations across actions and stages. The coroutine writes its result to `Temp/MatchFeedbackLiveResult.txt`; restart Play after running it.

Realms advances through Select Destination and Travel to Muster: there is no separate Gather Mana stage, and no attack stages of their own. `Tests/ManaTimingChecks.cs` checks this transition, funding during Muster and Events, and gathering (`GatherFor` taps the useful lands only; `PlayerMaterials.Shortfall` names what is owed). `Tests/DestinationChecks.cs` covers the settlement pool, land-gated choices, the single destination token, destination-gated plays and tapping, undo, persistence across turns and the opponent's travel preference.
